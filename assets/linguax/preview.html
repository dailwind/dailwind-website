Features Guide & Study Notes

Welcome to your comprehensive on-device linguistic companion. Learn how to leverage all advanced analysis modules and review complete study notes explaining every term below.

1 Loading Corpora & App Sharing (Corpus Tab)

Tap "Add files" or the dropzone to load .txt, .docx, or .pdf files from your device, or tap "Load sample corpus" for presets. External App Sharing: You can also share documents directly from WhatsApp, Telegram, Google Drive, or your device file manager into LinguaX Arc via the Android share sheet! The app cleans tokens, displays combined stats, word count (Tokens), unique vocabulary types, and rule-based Part-Of-Speech tagging estimates.

2 KWIC Concordance (KWIC Tab)

Enter any term (with wildcard *, ?, regex, and case options). Concordance lines align with the search word cleanly locked in the center column.
• Automatic 5-Second Center Alignment: Every concordance line is smoothly centered across all search results. Even when you swipe horizontally to read flanking context, all rows automatically snap back to perfect center alignment after 5 seconds.
• Instant Peek & Center Toolbar: Use ◀ Left, Right ▶, or the target Center button in the top banner to inspect surrounding words across all lines.
• KPF (Positional Pattern Frequency) Folding: Group identical grammatical sequences and rank patterns by frequency.
• Direct Document Jump: Tap "Open File ↗" on any concordance hit to immediately open that exact paragraph in the Book Reader File View reader.

3 Concordance Dispersion Plot (Plot Tab)

High-contrast Black & White Barcode Plot rendering solid black strikes for your search words on a pure white channel. Features horizontal scroll for full-length high-resolution document inspection and percentage milestone markers (0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 100%).

4 Book E-Reader & Full Screen (File View Tab)

Read loaded files in high-contrast, page-chunked sections (280 tokens per section) ensuring zero lag on large corpora.
• Corner Arrow Navigation: Use the corner accent arrow keys (◀ and ▶) at the far left and right edges, or the central page dropdown selector to jump between pages.
• Quick Percentage Jumps: Use the quick chips (Top 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, End 100%) to jump through the text.
• Match Highlights: Navigate occurrences with ◀ Prev Match and Next Match ▶ with live match counters and pulsing glowing highlights.

5 Collocational Clusters & Lexical Bundles (Clusters Tab)

Clusters search for multi-word recurrent frames surrounding a specific search word (sizes 2 to 5 words). Filter by minimum co-occurrence frequency to detect fixed idioms, formulaic constructions, and syntactic phrase patterns.

6 Contiguous N-Grams Sequences (N-Grams Tab)

N-Grams extract the most frequent contiguous sequences of words (lengths 2, 3, 4, and 5) across your entire loaded corpus without requiring a seed word. Configure minimum frequency thresholds to isolate recurrent stylistic sequences.

7 Collocation Association Statistics (Collocates Tab)

Measure statistical association (word attraction) between a node word and surrounding context words within a window of L5 to R5. Supports 5 association algorithms: Co-occurrence Frequency, Mutual Information (MI), Log-Likelihood (LL), T-Score, and Dice Coefficient to distinguish true linguistic partnerships from random chance.

8 Vocabulary Frequency Word Lists (Word List Tab)

Generate full frequency word lists of your corpus. Sortable by frequency or range (the number of separate files in which the word appears). Displays the absolute count and relative frequency normalized to per 1,000 words. Fully exportable to CSV.

9 Comparative Keyword Keyness (Keyword List Tab)

Compare your main corpus vocabulary with a Reference Corpus to extract unique statistically significant words. Employs Log-Likelihood (LL) for Keyness strength, and Log-Ratio for effect size. Discovers both Positive Keywords (overused) and Negative Keywords (underused).

10 Interactive Native Word Clouds (Word Cloud Tab)

Generates an interactive, zero-overlapping offline word canvas rendered natively on-device. Click on any word to instantly launch a KWIC concordance search! Save high-resolution PNG images directly to your device Gallery or share with colleagues.

11 Intelligent Sentiment & Tone Analyzer (Sentiment Tab)

Analyze sentiment polarity, emotive tone distribution, and compute scalar valence scores (-1.0 to 1.0) entirely on-device. Input your custom text or select loaded files from your corpus list to visualize the sentence-by-sentence emotive arc. Filter sentences by Positive/Negative polarity, and explore emotional categories (Joy, Sadness, Anger, Fear) shown via dynamic progress bars.

12 Academic Summarizer (AI Summarize Tab)

Generate deep, context-aware abstractive summaries, outline major research findings, isolate key arguments, and extract research methodologies from any file in your corpus. Powered by Gemini API to provide highly professional academic analysis.

13 Corpus Preprocessing & Conversion (Toolkit Tab)

Clean and normalize corpora, merge multiple text files into a single master document, and convert multi-format documents (including docx and pdf files) directly into plain txt formats for immediate downstream analysis.

14 Word Count & Quick Navigation Search

Tap the search pill or magnifying glass in the application header to search word counts across your corpus in real time, or type tool names to jump directly to any module. Press Enter to instantly go to the top matching tool!

15 Offline Research Workspace & Academic Citation

All analysis sessions are logged in your local Workspace History with instant parameter restore and full data exports (CSV, TSV, JSON, Markdown). Open the Citation modal to copy official APA 7th, MLA 9th, or Chicago 17th format references with Zenodo DOI records.

Corpus Linguistics Study Notes & Glossary

Easy study notes explaining every corpus setting, parameter, formula, and table column in clear bullets with real-world examples.

Search & Pattern Settings

  • Case Sensitive: Controls whether uppercase and lowercase letters are treated as different words.
    • Case OFF (Default): Searching apple matches apple, Apple, and APPLE.
    • Case ON: Searching apple matches ONLY small letters. Use this to separate proper nouns (like Brown the surname, Bush the politician) from regular nouns (brown color, bush plant).
  • Regex (Regular Expressions): Pattern-matching formulas for searching grammatical variations and morphological affixes.
    • play(ed|ing|s)? matches play, plays, played, and playing in one search.
    • \bpre\w+ finds all words starting with the prefix "pre-" (e.g. predict, prevent).
    • colou?r matches both British colour and American color.
  • Wildcards (* and ?): Easy letter placeholders.
    • Asterisk (*): Zero or more letters (e.g. teach* matches teach, teacher, teaching).
    • Question Mark (?): Exactly one character (e.g. w?man matches woman and women).

KWIC Concordance, Spans & Context

  • KWIC (Key Word In Context) & Node Word: Foundational corpus linguistics view aligning all occurrences of your searched term (the Node Word) in a vertical center column with flanking context words. In LinguaX Arc, rows automatically re-center after 5 seconds so the searched node word remains the visual anchor.
  • Context Size / Window: The number of words gathered to the left and right (±5 to ±25 words). 5 words isolates immediate grammar; 15–25 words displays full sentences.
  • Left Span (L1–L5) & Right Span (R1–R5): Exact position relative to the search word.
    Sentence: "She drank a glass of cold [WATER] from the tap"
    • L1 (1 word left): cold (Adjective modifying water)
    • L2 (2 words left): of | L3 (3 words left): glass
    • R1 (1 word right): from (Preposition governing water) | R2: the | R3: tap
  • Sort by Context: Organizes concordance lines alphabetically based on words in a specific position (e.g. sorting by R1 groups all instances of interested in, interested to, interested by into clean consecutive blocks).

Frequency, Thresholds & Diversity

  • Tokens vs. Types vs. TTR %:
    • Tokens (Running Words): Total word count in text, including every repetition.
    • Types (Unique Words): Distinct vocabulary words with duplicates removed.
    • TTR % (Type-Token Ratio): (Types / Tokens) × 100. High TTR (70%+) indicates rich, diverse vocabulary; low TTR (under 30%) indicates repetitive language.
  • Minimum Frequency (Min Freq / Min F): Cutoff threshold that hides any word, phrase, or collocate appearing fewer times than this number. Setting Min Freq = 3 excludes typos and one-off accidental words (hapax legomena).
  • Sort by Minimum (Threshold Sorting & Ordering): Organizes table results based on the qualifying minimum frequency boundary. Allows researchers to rank results starting from the lowest qualifying threshold upward (ascending order) to inspect rare, borderline lexical bundles that would otherwise be hidden under high-frequency words.
  • Core Frequency: The baseline total frequency a word must achieve across the entire corpus before being evaluated as an association collocate or keyword candidate. Prevents rare typos from receiving false 100% association scores.
  • Range & Range %:
    • Range: Number of separate corpus files containing the item.
    • Range %: (Range / Total Files) × 100. If Range is 90%, the word is used across the whole corpus; if Range is 1 file, it is an idiosyncratic habit of that single author.

Clusters, N-Grams & Collocations

  • Size & Side (Clusters / Lexical Bundles):
    • Size: Phrase length (2-word, 3-word, 4-word, or 5-word phrases).
    • Side: Where adjoining words are gathered relative to the seed word: Left (before), Right (after), or Both (flanking).
    • Example for "conclusion": Size 3 Left = in the conclusion; Size 3 Right = conclusion of the.
  • Function Words vs. Content Words (& Stopwords):
    • Function Words (Grammar): Articles (the, a), prepositions (in, of), pronouns (he, it), conjunctions (and, but). Filtered out via Stopwords in Word Lists to focus on content words.
    • Content Words (Lexical): Nouns (climate, treaty), main verbs (negotiate), adjectives (crucial), adverbs (rapidly). Carry the topical message.
  • Clusters vs. N-Grams: Clusters are multi-word frames anchored to a specific search word (e.g. "as a matter of fact" around "matter"); N-Grams are all frequent contiguous sequences across the entire corpus without needing any starting seed word.
  • Collocations & Co-occurrence Frequency (O11): The habitual attraction between words (e.g. heavy rain vs strong rain). Co-occurrence (O11) is the count of times the collocate appears in the ±5 word span of the node word.
  • Association Statistics (MI, T-Score, Dice):
    • Mutual Information (MI ≥ 3.0): Measures exclusivity and bond strength. Highlights idioms and specialized partnerships.
    • T-Score (≥ 2.0): Measures statistical certainty and confidence, favoring frequent grammatical partnerships.
    • Dice Coefficient (0.0 to 1.0): Harmonic exclusivity score, immune to corpus length differences.

Comparative Keyness & Significance

  • Positive Keywords (Statistically Overused): Words whose frequency in your target study corpus is statistically far higher than in the reference corpus (Log Ratio > 0). They reveal the core genre, topic, and style (e.g. in a medical corpus: patient, clinical, dosage).
  • Negative Keywords (Statistically Underused): Words whose frequency in your target corpus is statistically far lower than in the reference corpus (Log Ratio < 0), or completely absent! They reveal stylistic taboos or features systematically avoided (e.g. academic articles avoid casual pronouns like I, you, me, wanna). "Negative" means mathematical underuse, NOT negative sentiment!
  • Log-Likelihood (LL / G²): Gold-standard significance test in corpus linguistics. Accurately handles non-normal, skewed textual distributions where rare words are common (Zipf's law).
  • Chi-Square (χ²): Classical contingency table test comparing observed vs. expected counts. Overestimates significance on words with low expected counts (< 5).
  • Log Ratio (Effect Size & Magnitude): Measures how big the proportional difference is:
    • +1.0: Word is 2 times (200%) more frequent in target corpus.
    • +2.0: Word is 4 times (400%) more frequent in target corpus.
    • +3.0: Word is 8 times (800%) more frequent in target corpus.
    • -1.0: Word is half as frequent (50%) in target corpus.
  • Significance Cutoff & p-values (Critical Values Table):
    • LL / χ² ≥ 3.84: Significant at p < 0.05 (95% confidence).
    • LL / χ² ≥ 6.63: Highly significant at p < 0.01 (99% confidence).
    • LL / χ² ≥ 10.83: Very highly significant at p < 0.001 (99.9% confidence).
    • LL / χ² ≥ 15.13: Overwhelmingly significant at p < 0.0001 (99.99% confidence).

Master Table Columns Legend (All Tables Explained)

  • Hit #: The sequential concordance match index (Hit 1, Hit 2...).
  • File / Source: Specific document in your loaded corpus where the occurrence was found.
  • Left / Node / Right: In KWIC: Node is your keyword; Left is preceding sentence context; Right is following sentence context.
  • Rank: Table position in the sorted list (Rank 1 = most frequent word or top statistical score).
  • Freq (Raw Freq): Raw count of how many times the word or phrase occurred.
  • TGT Frequency: Raw count of the word in your main Study / Target Corpus.
  • TGT 1k (Per 1k): Normalized frequency per 1,000 words in Target Corpus: (TGT Freq / Total Words) × 1,000. Standardizes counts across texts of different lengths!
  • REF Frequency: Raw count in your comparison Reference Corpus.
  • REF 1k: Normalized frequency per 1,000 words in Reference Corpus: (REF Freq / Total Reference Words) × 1,000.
  • Keyness (LL): Log-Likelihood score. Values above 3.84, 6.63, and 10.83 represent 95%, 99%, and 99.9% statistical significance.
  • Chi-Square (χ²): Classical keyness score comparing observed vs expected frequencies.
  • Log Ratio: Effect size (+1.0 = twice as frequent; +2.0 = 4x; -1.0 = half as frequent).
  • Keyword Status: Positive (Overused) or Negative (Underused) in the study corpus.
  • Range & Range %: Number and percentage of separate corpus files containing the word.
  • Co-occur (O11): In Collocates: Observed co-occurrences in the ±5 word span of the node word.
  • Stat Score: Mathematical score of the selected association formula (MI, T-Score, LL, or Dice).
  • Span (L5..L1, R1..R5): Exact count of occurrences at each individual position around the node.
  • Tokens & Types: Running words count (Tokens) vs unique distinct vocabulary words (Types).
  • TTR %: Type-Token Ratio (Types / Tokens) × 100 measuring lexical diversity.
  • Page # & Doc %: In File View and Dispersion Plots: 280-token chunk page and 0–100% position in text.
  • Valence, Polarity & Emotion: Sentiment score (-1.00 to +1.00), category (Positive/Negative/Neutral), and Joy/Sadness/Anger/Fear breakdown.
