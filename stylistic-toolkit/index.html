04 / KNOW THE METHOD

A companion to interpretation.

Foregrounding involves patterns of parallelism and deviation in relation to a norm. Counts and surface patterns can guide close reading; they cannot establish literary effect or authorial intent.

Scope: Rule-based English cues, not an AI critic or a full linguistic parser. Unicode text is accepted, but English cue lists do not generalize to all languages. For automatic syntax/grammar patterns supply CoNLL-U annotations. Phonological detection uses only words with pronunciation hints; no automatic phonemic transcription, stress or meter. Semantic and pragmatic results require human interpretation.

Descriptive frequency contrasts are not significance tests. Reference differences can reflect topic, genre or corpus size. Low-frequency words are excluded from automatic reference contrasts. Units follow punctuation and line breaks, so abbreviations can create extra boundaries.

Conceptual reading: Miall & Kuiken (1994), Foregrounding, defamiliarization, and affect; Lancaster University foregrounding checksheet. These sources inform the approach; they do not validate this implementation.
